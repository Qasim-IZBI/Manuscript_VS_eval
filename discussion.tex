\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The scaling study across 54 configurations and the subsequent ensemble analysis reveal
that the optimal scaling point is model-family-specific and non-trivially predictable
from perceptual metrics alone, and that uncertainty calibration is contingent on
architectural properties that differ substantially across families.

\paragraph{Perceptual metric saturation and the indispensability of task-based evaluation.}
Perceptual metrics saturate within a narrow band across well-behaved GAN configurations
(Patch-SSIM $0.30$--$0.35$, LPIPS $0.037$--$0.040$, FID $190$--$215$), with Spearman
rank correlations against CPA~MAE of only $\rho{=}0.63$, $0.64$, and $0.55$.
As a concrete illustration, CycleGAN\,M/100\% and CycleGAN\,L/100\% are nearly
indistinguishable by Patch-SSIM ($0.334$ vs.\ $0.342$) yet their CPA~MAE differs by
more than an order of magnitude ($0.008$ vs.\ $0.085$) — a discrepancy that would
translate to systematic fibrosis staging bias.
These findings confirm that task-based evaluation is non-negotiable for virtual staining
model selection~\cite{trustworthy_i2i2025}.

\paragraph{Practical implications of scaling: minimum viable configurations.}
The CPA~MAE landscape is non-smooth: isolated low-error cells appear within otherwise
moderate surfaces rather than monotonically improving with scale, partly due to the high
variance of CPA~MAE over $N{=}5$ test WSIs.
When the full training set is available, medium-capacity generators are the most reliable
choice across families.
MUNIT is the most stable family overall (CPA~MAE $0.040$--$0.059$ across all nine
conditions), consistent with AdaIN-based style disentanglement acting as an implicit
regulariser.
DCLGAN\,S is competitive across all data fractions (CPA~MAE $0.028$--$0.049$), making
it a sound choice under limited data.
CycleGAN and UNIT show non-monotonic data-fraction dependence and are less reliable when
training data is scarce.

\paragraph{Training instability as a deployment risk.}
DCLGAN and UVCGAN each exhibit catastrophic failure modes that are invisible until they
occur.
DCLGAN's large generator collapses at reduced data fractions (FID $327$--$363$,
Patch-SSIM $0.13$--$0.14$) while smaller variants remain stable, reflecting insufficient
patch density for the dual InfoNCE contrastive buffer.
UVCGAN's two-stage schedule~\cite{torbunov2022uvcgan} does not scale beyond the small
generator in this data regime; medium and large variants fail across most data fractions
(FID up to $395$, Patch-SSIM as low as $0.14$).
Both failures manifest as sharp discontinuities rather than gradual degradation, so
monitoring FID and Patch-SSIM throughout training is essential for early detection.

\paragraph{CycleDiffusion as a distinct generative paradigm.}
CycleDiffusion's consistently lower Patch-SSIM ($0.200$--$0.293$ vs.\ $0.298$--$0.349$
for stable GANs) and higher LPIPS reflect the DDIM inversion inductive bias rather
than failure: the noise-space bottleneck enforces domain-invariant structure at the cost
of per-tile pixel sharpness.
The small generator's CPA~MAE rises sharply with data reduction ($0.051$ at 100\%
$\to$ $0.171$ at 25\%), consistent with DDIM inversion requiring well-covered training
distributions to produce stable noise representations.
Counter-intuitively, the medium generator achieves its lowest CPA~MAE at 25\% data
($0.035$ vs.\ $0.082$ at 100\%), suggesting limited diversity acts as a regulariser
against slide-specific colour artefacts.
At 100\% data, the small generator (CPA~MAE $0.051$) is selected for ensemble
uncertainty estimation; the medium generator is preferable under data reduction.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The dataset covers a single tissue type, disease model, and staining laboratory;
multi-centre validation on human liver tissue is required to establish generalisability.
The held-out test set of $N{=}5$ WSIs provides limited statistical power: configurations
with CPA~MAE $0.008$ carry a standard deviation of $0.007$, so fine-grained rankings
among the best configurations should be interpreted with caution.
Patch-SSIM and LPIPS are computed on loosely registered adjacent-section pairs,
inflating apparent perceptual error for structurally correct but misaligned translations.
The uncertainty framework captures epistemic uncertainty only; aleatoric uncertainty
from staining variability and biological heterogeneity between sections is not modelled.
Finally, ensemble-size sensitivity was not ablated, and ten members may be insufficient
for large or highly non-convex generators.
