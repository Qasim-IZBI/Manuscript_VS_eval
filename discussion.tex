\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

This study has addressed two complementary questions: which unsupervised I2I
configuration yields the most faithful H\&E~$\to$~SR virtual stain for quantitative
collagen assessment, and whether deep ensemble uncertainty can reliably flag regions
of low translational confidence without access to pixel-level ground truth.
The scaling study across 54 configurations and the subsequent ensemble analysis
together reveal that answering the first question requires task-based evaluation,
that the optimal scaling point is model-family-specific and non-trivially predictable
from perceptual metrics alone, and that the second question is contingent on
architectural properties that differ substantially across families.

\paragraph{Perceptual metric saturation and the indispensability of task-based evaluation.}
The most consequential methodological finding is that perceptual and distributional
metrics saturate within a narrow band across the majority of well-behaved GAN
configurations: Patch-SSIM spans only $0.30$--$0.35$, LPIPS $0.037$--$0.040$, and FID
$190$--$215$ across the corresponding stable configurations.
Spearman rank correlations between these perceptual orderings and the CPA MAE ordering
are only $\rho{=}0.63$, $0.64$, and $0.55$ respectively.
The practical consequence is direct: a practitioner selecting model configurations
based on Patch-SSIM or LPIPS alone would have no reliable means to identify the best
configuration for collagen quantification.
As a concrete example, CycleGAN\,M/100\% and CycleGAN\,L/100\% produce nearly
identical Patch-SSIM ($0.334$ vs.\ $0.342$) and LPIPS ($0.038$), yet their CPA MAE
values differ by more than an order of magnitude ($0.008$ vs.\ $0.085$), a discrepancy
that would translate to a systematic bias in fibrosis staging.
Patch-SSIM and LPIPS rankings are therefore only moderately predictive of CPA MAE
rankings, and optimising for either perceptual metric is a necessary but not sufficient
condition for low CPA MAE.
These findings reinforce the case for task-based evaluation as a non-negotiable
component of any virtual staining benchmark~\cite{trustworthy_i2i2025}.

\paragraph{Practical implications of scaling: minimum viable configurations.}
The CPA MAE landscape across the $3{\times}3$ scaling grids is non-smooth: isolated
low-error cells appear within otherwise moderate surfaces rather than monotonically
improving with scale.
This pattern reflects sensitivity to how the specific training slides in each
data-fraction subset sample the fibrosis distribution, amplified by the small held-out
test set ($N{=}5$ WSIs) which gives CPA MAE estimates high variance.
For UNIT and CycleGAN specifically, some generator sizes achieve lower CPA MAE at
reduced data fractions than at 100\%, a counter-intuitive result that is most plausibly
explained by the high variance of CPA MAE over five test WSIs rather than a genuine
regularisation benefit of withheld data.
Despite this, some practically useful generalisations hold across families.
When the full training set is available, medium-capacity generators are the most
reliable choice: CycleGAN\,M, UNIT\,M, and MUNIT\,M all achieve their best CPA MAE at
100\% data.
MUNIT is the most stable family overall, with all nine configurations spanning a
narrow CPA MAE range of $0.040$--$0.059$ and no generator size yielding a systematic
advantage; this insensitivity to both capacity and data scaling is consistent with
AdaIN-based style disentanglement acting as an implicit regulariser on the generator.
DCLGAN\,S is stable and competitive at all three data fractions (CPA MAE
$0.028$--$0.049$), making it a reliable choice even when training data is limited.
CycleGAN and UNIT show non-monotonic data-fraction dependence that makes their
behaviour at reduced data fractions harder to predict, and are therefore not
recommended when training data is scarce.

\paragraph{Training instability as a deployment risk.}
Two families exhibit catastrophic failure modes under specific scaling conditions, and
both failures are invisible until they occur.
DCLGAN's dual patch-level InfoNCE contrastive objective requires a sufficient density
of training patches to populate the contrastive memory buffer at high generator
capacity: reducing the data fraction to 50\% or 25\% causes the large-capacity variant
to collapse (FID $327$--$363$, Patch-SSIM $0.13$--$0.14$) while leaving smaller
variants unaffected.
UVCGAN's two-stage schedule, masked-image pretraining followed by cycle-consistent
fine-tuning~\cite{torbunov2022uvcgan}, does not scale gracefully beyond the small
generator in this dataset regime: medium and large variants fail across most data
fractions, achieving FID as high as $395$ and Patch-SSIM as low as $0.14$.
The most practically important observation is that both failure modes manifest as
sharp discontinuities in the scaling grid rather than gradual degradation, meaning
that an evaluation conducted at a single (model size, data fraction) point can give a
misleadingly optimistic or pessimistic impression of a family's capabilities.
Monitoring FID and Patch-SSIM jointly throughout training, rather than relying on
training loss alone, is essential for detecting these failures early.

\paragraph{CycleDiffusion as a distinct generative paradigm.}
CycleDiffusion's behaviour diverges from GAN-based families along several dimensions
that are attributable to its shared DDPM noise space~\cite{wu2022cyclediffusion} rather
than to failure.
Its consistently lower Patch-SSIM ($0.200$--$0.293$ vs.\ $0.298$--$0.349$ for stable
GANs) and higher LPIPS reflect the different inductive bias of DDPM generation: the
DDIM inversion bottleneck enforces domain-invariant structure at the cost of per-tile
pixel sharpness relative to an adversarially trained generator.
The small generator's extreme sensitivity to data fraction (CPA MAE rising from
$0.051$ at 100\% data to $0.171$ at 25\%) reflects the dependence of DDIM inversion
on stable noise representations that require well-covered training distributions to
learn; insufficient coverage causes the inversion to map test tiles to out-of-distribution
noise codes, producing unreliable translations even when the forward DDPM loss has
converged.
The medium generator achieves its lowest CPA MAE at the 25\% data fraction ($0.035$)
rather than at 100\% ($0.082$); this counter-intuitive outcome is consistent with
limited training-set diversity acting as a regulariser that prevents the model from
overfitting stain-specific colour or texture artefacts in individual training slides.
When the full training set is available, the small generator yields the lowest CPA
MAE for CycleDiffusion ($0.051$) and is therefore selected for ensemble uncertainty
estimation; the medium generator is a more capable option at reduced data fractions.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Several limitations bound the generalisability and precision of the conclusions.

The dataset comprises a single tissue type~(mouse liver), a single disease model
(bile-duct ligation), and a single staining laboratory; generalisability to human liver
tissue, other fibrosis aetiologies, or slides acquired with different scanners or
staining protocols is unknown and requires multi-centre validation.

The held-out test set consists of $N{=}5$ WSIs, providing limited statistical power for
CPA MAE estimation: configurations with a headline MAE of $0.008$ carry a standard
deviation of $0.007$, making the apparent ordering between the very best configurations
sensitive to the specific test slides included.
A formal sample-size analysis was not conducted prior to the study; future benchmarks
in this setting should target at least 15--20 test WSIs for stable CPA MAE estimation.

H\&E and Sirius Red sections are cut from the same tissue block but are physically
distinct sections, introducing irremovable spatial misalignment from tissue deformation.
This precludes pixel-level paired ground truth and means that Patch-SSIM and LPIPS
are computed on loosely registered tile pairs, inflating apparent perceptual error
for translations that are structurally correct but misaligned.

The uncertainty framework estimates epistemic uncertainty only.
Aleatoric uncertainty arising from staining variability, section thickness differences,
and biological heterogeneity between adjacent sections is not modelled, meaning that
the uncertainty maps cannot distinguish between regions that are genuinely ambiguous
in the H\&E input and regions that are consistently misstained across ensemble members.

Finally, ten ensemble members may be insufficient to resolve uncertainty variance for
large or highly non-convex models; the ensemble-size sensitivity was not ablated in
this study.

\paragraph{Future directions.}
The most immediate extension is multi-centre validation on human liver tissue with
standardised fibrosis stage annotations~(Ishak or METAVIR) from multiple institutions
and scanners, which would establish whether the scaling and stability findings
generalise beyond the current single-lab mouse dataset.

Registration-assisted evaluation, using deformable image registration to align
adjacent H\&E and SR sections at the patch level, would replace the loose-registration
compromise currently adopted for Patch-SSIM and LPIPS and enable more direct
per-tile paired ground truth for calibration.

Decomposing predictive uncertainty into epistemic and aleatoric components,
for example via a latent variable model layered over the ensemble~\cite{kendall2017uncertainties},
would allow pathologists to distinguish regions that require more training data from
regions that are inherently ambiguous due to staining variability.

Replacing convolutional generator backbones with pre-trained histopathology foundation
models~(e.g., UNI, CONCH) could mitigate data-fraction sensitivity by providing
a strong morphological prior, which may be particularly beneficial for CycleDiffusion
and UVCGAN where large-capacity generators currently underperform in limited-data
regimes.

Finally, extending the benchmark to include additional histochemical stains used in
liver fibrosis assessment, such as Masson's trichrome and periodic acid--Schiff, would
establish whether the relative model rankings reported here are stain-specific or
reflect more general H\&E translation characteristics.
