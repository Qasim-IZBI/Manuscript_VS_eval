\section{Epistemic Uncertainty Analysis}
\label{sec:uncertainty}

The scaling study~(Section~\ref{sec:results_scaling}) identifies how model capacity and
data volume jointly affect translation quality, but it treats each configuration as a
deterministic predictor.
In an unpaired setting, no ground-truth target constrains the generator: a model may
produce a stain that appears visually plausible yet does not faithfully reproduce the
underlying tissue structure, a hallucination that is indistinguishable from a correct
translation in a single forward pass.
This is a critical concern for clinical adoption, as a hallucinated collagen pattern
carries the same visual appearance as a true one.
We address this gap by training deep ensembles for the best-performing generator size
from each model family at the full training data volume, for reasons detailed in
Section~\ref{sec:ens_selection}, and constructing spatially resolved epistemic
uncertainty maps that flag regions of high inter-member disagreement, enabling
pathologists to review low-confidence areas with appropriate caution.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Model Selection for Ensemble Analysis}
\label{sec:ens_selection}
% -------------------------------------------------------

For each family we select the generator size trained on the full training set (100\%
data fraction) with the lowest CPA~MAE; FID serves as a secondary tiebreak.
Fixing the data fraction ensures that ensemble uncertainty differences reflect
architectural properties rather than training volume.
Selected configurations are marked with a star ($\bigstar$) in
Figure~\ref{fig:results_overview}.

\textbf{Ensemble training:}
For each selected configuration, ten models are trained independently from distinct
random seeds, using the identical hyperparameters and training data as the corresponding
scaling-study run.
The ensemble size of $K{=}10$ is motivated by convergence analysis showing that
calibration errors plateau at approximately ten members for deep ensembles in
biomedical image segmentation~\cite{siddiqui2024uncertainty}, and is consistent
with prior ensemble-based uncertainty work in virtual staining
applications~\cite{trustworthy_cell_ensemble2023}.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification}
\label{sec:unc_maps}
% -------------------------------------------------------

For each selected configuration, the ensemble mean image is the pixel-wise average
of the $K{=}10$ generator outputs; the epistemic uncertainty map
$\mathbf{U}(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$ is the sum of per-channel sample variances
across ensemble members (unbiased, $K{-}1$ denominator)~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple}.
Summing channels preserves the total disagreement signal; high values flag pixels where
ensemble members disagree --- due to ambiguous texture, rare morphologies, or
out-of-distribution artefacts.
A tile-level scalar $\bar{\sigma}^2(x)$ is the spatial mean of $\mathbf{U}(x)$ over
tissue pixels, used in the calibration analyses below.
Full equations are given in Supplementary Sec.~9.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Uncertainty Calibration}
\label{sec:unc_calibration}
% -------------------------------------------------------

A spatially resolved uncertainty map is only useful if it is \textit{calibrated}:
when the model assigns high uncertainty to a pixel, the translation should be
measurably worse at that pixel.
Verifying this requires an independent per-pixel error signal.
In a supervised setting this would be the absolute deviation from a known target;
in the unpaired setting of this work no such target exists, because the H\&E and SR
images originate from different physical sections.
We therefore use \textit{cycle-reconstruction error} as a proxy for per-pixel
translation difficulty.
MC~dropout~\cite{gal2016dropout} was not adopted as an alternative to deep ensembles
because its uncertainty estimates require extensive dropout-rate tuning to achieve
calibration, and miscalibration persists even after tuning in complex
tasks~\cite{siddiqui2024uncertainty,trustworthy_i2i2025}; deep ensembles yield
well-calibrated estimates with minimal hyperparameter
sensitivity~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple,siddiqui2024uncertainty}.

\textbf{Calibration metrics:}
Calibration is evaluated against cycle-reconstruction error $E(x,y) = |A(x,y) -
G_{B\to A}(G_{A\to B}(A))(x,y)|$ as a proxy for per-pixel translation difficulty
(ground-truth pixel targets are unavailable in the unpaired setting).
Three complementary metrics are computed over tissue pixels (full definitions in
Supplementary Sec.~9): (i)~within-tile Spearman~$\rho$ between $U$ and $E$ over tissue
pixels~(spatial calibration); (ii)~across-tile Spearman and Pearson~$\rho$ between
tile-mean $\overline{U}$ and tile-mean $\overline{E}$~(tile-level calibration, catching
the failure mode where a model assigns uniform uncertainty across tiles); and
(iii)~pixel-level and tile-level ECE~\cite{guo2017ece} computed after jointly rescaling
$U$ and $E$ to $[0,1]$ via dataset-global $p_1$--$p_{99}$ bounds.
Cycle-reconstruction error is a proxy: self-cycle error can be underestimated when
forward and inverse generators share systematic biases, and cross-section biological
variation between H\&E and SR sections adds an irreducible ECE noise floor.

\textbf{Within-tile spatial calibration.}
All six families produce weak within-tile Spearman correlations ($\rho = 0.058$--$0.165$),
indicating that high-uncertainty pixels are only loosely co-located with high-error
pixels within a tile.
MUNIT ($\rho = 0.165 \pm 0.173$) and DCLGAN ($\rho = 0.157 \pm 0.084$) are the
strongest, though MUNIT's large standard deviation reflects marked tile-to-tile
inconsistency.
UVCGAN ($\rho = 0.069$) and CycleDiffusion ($\rho = 0.058$) show near-zero spatial
calibration.
These values rule out pixel-level uncertainty as a reliable quality gate
(per-family $\rho$ distributions are shown in Supplementary Sec.~9.3).

\textbf{Across-tile calibration.}
GAN-based families exhibit moderate across-tile Pearson correlations between tile-mean
uncertainty and tile-mean error ($r = 0.422$--$0.605$), with DCLGAN and MUNIT ranking
highest.
The across-tile Spearman correlations are substantially weaker and inconsistent
($\rho = {-}0.163$--$0.206$), revealing that the linear covariation is driven by a
small number of high-leverage tiles rather than a robust monotonic ordering.
CycleDiffusion is wholly uncorrelated (Pearson $r = {-}0.027$, Spearman $\rho = {-}0.038$);
per-family scatter plots are shown in Supplementary Sec.~9.3.

\textbf{Reliability diagrams and ECE.}
DCLGAN achieves the lowest tile-level ECE ($0.081$; Figure~\ref{fig:calibration_reliability}),
followed by MUNIT ($0.097$); UNIT is the worst ($0.260$).
All six reliability curves lie predominantly \emph{below} the identity line: ensembles
assign higher uncertainty than the normalised cycle error warrants.
This systematic bias is partly structural — all models minimise cycle-reconstruction
loss, compressing the error proxy relative to inter-member disagreement.
The lowest-uncertainty bin is an exception for CycleGAN, MUNIT, and DCLGAN, where
error exceeds uncertainty, confirming that high-confidence tiles are not guaranteed to
be error-free.

\textbf{CycleDiffusion: categorically uninformative uncertainty.}
CycleDiffusion's reliability diagram presents a flat error line across all ten uncertainty
bins ($\overline{E_n} \approx 0.44$--$0.49$, constant), with near-zero across-tile
correlation in both Pearson and Spearman.
The underlying cause is the DDIM inversion cycle error, which is an order of magnitude
larger than for GAN families (tile-mean $p_1$--$p_{99}$: 30.0--38.6 vs.\ 0.7--6.7
for GAN models in unnormalised units) and varies so little across tiles that it
cannot be predicted from ensemble variance.
The uncertainty signal therefore carries no information about translation quality for
CycleDiffusion.

\textbf{Summary.}
GAN-based ensembles produce a weak but non-trivial tile-level triage signal (across-tile
Pearson 0.42--0.61), with DCLGAN and MUNIT as the most reliable candidates.
No family achieves meaningful within-tile spatial calibration, and CycleDiffusion
uncertainty is wholly uninformative.
These results indicate that task-based CPA MAE evaluation cannot be replaced by
uncertainty screening alone.
