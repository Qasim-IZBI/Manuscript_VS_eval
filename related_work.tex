\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Virtual Staining in Histopathology}

Histopathological diagnosis relies on a panel of stains applied to thin tissue sections,
each requiring dedicated reagents, skilled technicians, and additional biopsy material.
Virtual staining---the computational prediction of a target stain from an existing one
using deep learning---offers a pathway to reduce tissue consumption, laboratory costs,
and turn-around time while standardising stain quality across
centres~\cite{rivenson2020emerging, latonen2024virtual}.
In liver pathology, Sirius Red~(SR) staining is the clinical reference for collagen
quantification and fibrosis staging; obtaining it requires a separate section from the
same biopsy as the routinely acquired haematoxylin and eosin~(H\&E) slide.
Early virtual staining approaches relied on supervised pixel-to-pixel translation between
registered image pairs~\cite{isola2017image}, but obtaining geometrically aligned adjacent
sections is impractical at scale due to tissue deformation and processing artefacts,
motivating the shift towards unsupervised, unpaired learning
frameworks~\cite{aatresh2024virtual, chen2025challenges}.

\subsection{Unsupervised Image-to-Image Translation}

Cycle-consistent adversarial networks~(CycleGAN;~\cite{zhu2017unpaired}) established the
dominant paradigm for unpaired stain translation by training two generators with a
cycle-consistency loss that enforces invertibility without pixel-level supervision.
UNIT~\cite{liu2017unsupervised} extended this idea with a shared VAE bottleneck,
encouraging both domains to occupy a common latent space and enabling more coherent
cross-domain mappings.
MUNIT~\cite{huang2018multimodal} introduced a content--style disentanglement, separating
domain-invariant spatial structure from domain-specific appearance via Adaptive Instance
Normalisation, enabling multimodal generation from a single source image.
DCLGAN~\cite{han2021dual} augmented the CycleGAN framework with a dual contrastive
objective that maximises mutual information between corresponding patches in both
translation directions, improving embedding quality without increasing generator
complexity.
UVCGAN~\cite{torbunov2022uvcgan} revisited cycle-consistent training with a
UNet--Vision Transformer hybrid generator and self-supervised masked image pretraining,
achieving stronger preservation of long-range structural correlations.
More recently, score-based diffusion models have been applied to this problem:
Liu~\etal~\cite{liu2025score} proposed a mutual-information-guided reverse diffusion
process with timestep-customised noise scheduling and a local patch-contrastive loss for
unpaired H\&E\,$\to$\,IHC translation, offering finer control over staining intensity
and structural reconstruction than adversarial methods alone.

\subsection{Applications to Histopathology Stain Translation}

These architectures have been adopted for a growing range of histopathological stain
conversion tasks.
CycleGAN remains the most widely deployed backbone: Liu~\etal~\cite{liu2021virtual}
reported the first virtual Ki-67 IHC staining from H\&E in breast cancer, incorporating
a pathological representation network to penalise deviations in tumour-cell spatial
predictions between the forward and backward generators.
MVFStain~\cite{chen2022mvfstain} drew on MUNIT's style--content separation to train a
single network producing nine IHC and functional stains from H\&E across lung and breast
tissue.
In liver histopathology, an early large-scale validation study~\cite{kieffer2020largescale}
% [TBC]: verify first author of the Modern Pathology 2020 NASH trichrome paper
demonstrated that an unsupervised GAN could translate H\&E to Masson's trichrome on
$n{=}574$ NASH whole-slide images~(WSIs), with pathologists unable to distinguish virtual
from chemically stained slides in a Turing test.
A subsequent prior-guided GAN~(PG-GAN;~\cite{li2023unpaired})
% [TBC]: verify first author of the Medical Image Analysis 2023 PG-GAN paper
embedded pathology-feature constraints directly into the generator for
H\&E\,$\to$\,trichrome translation on liver biopsy WSIs, achieving a Spearman
correlation of $\rho{=}0.82$ between virtual and real stain fibrosis scores.
DCLGAN was applied to a bespoke virtual staining task by Adib~\etal~\cite{adib2024dual},
translating autofluorescence images of unstained skin tissue to H\&E with 90.2\%
diagnostic concordance among dermatopathologists.
UVCGAN has been included as a strong unpaired baseline in multi-domain staining studies,
though recent comparisons for H\&E\,$\to$\,fibre-stain translation~\cite{vifit2025} found
it struggled to generate valid fibre texture.
Despite these advances, no study to date has directly compared these six model families
on unpaired H\&E\,$\to$\,histochemical stain translation in mouse liver tissue.

\subsection{Evaluation Frameworks for Virtual Staining}

Evaluating virtual staining models poses unique challenges.
Pixel-level metrics~(SSIM, PSNR, MAE) require co-registered ground-truth pairs, while
distributional metrics such as FID and KID measure population-level realism without
assessing spatial correspondence.
Khan~\etal~\cite{khan2023effect} showed that standard image quality metrics do not always
correlate with histological feasibility in virtual H\&E staining, underscoring the need
for pathology-aware evaluation.
VISGAB~\cite{visgab2025} introduced a histology-specific fidelity index~(HSFI)---derived
from pathologist concordance experiments on skin tissue---and found that CycleGAN
outperforms DCLGAN and CUTGAN on structural fidelity despite slower inference.
Kl\"{o}ckner~\etal~\cite{klockner2025he} benchmarked paired and unpaired models for
breast cancer IHC generation on the BCI and MIST datasets, reporting that quantitative
rankings frequently disagree with qualitative pathologist assessments.
The StainDiffuser benchmark~\cite{staindiffuser2024} is the most comprehensive to date,
evaluating more than twenty GAN-based and diffusion-based I2I models with a consistent
metric suite.
Our study contributes a complementary evaluation focused on an underexplored histochemical
stain target in mouse liver tissue from a BDL model of cholestatic liver disease---Sirius
Red~(SR) for collagen and fibrosis quantification---using a dataset collected in-house,
and assesses all six models using both patch-level and distributional metrics.

A growing body of work argues that image quality metrics alone are insufficient and
advocates for \textit{task-based evaluation}, in which downstream clinical tasks are
applied to virtually generated images to test whether diagnostically relevant content
is faithfully reproduced.
This paradigm has been applied in several staining contexts.
In renal histopathology, a recent study used CycleGAN-based H\&E\,$\to$\,PAS virtual
staining as a prerequisite for glomerular segmentation across multiple
centres~\cite{renal_seg2025}: segmentation models trained on real PAS slides were
applied to virtually stained test images, and the resulting glomerular detection
performance served as the primary quality criterion.
In the IHC domain, task-based evaluation has been formalised for HER2 scoring:
virtual H\&E\,$\to$\,HER2 IHC images were assessed not by pixel similarity but by
whether a downstream HER2 classifier produced concordant scores with those obtained on
real IHC~\cite{her2task2024}.
For collagen quantification specifically, deep learning models have been developed to
segment positive Sirius Red areas in liver WSIs and score fibrosis severity~\cite{sirius_fibrosis2024},
and U-Net-based collagen segmentation on Sirius Red achieves Dice scores of
$0.83$--$0.90$ across heterogeneous staining conditions~\cite{liverfibrosis2025}.
We adopt this task-based paradigm by applying an SR-specific collagen segmentation model
to both real and virtually generated SR images and comparing the resulting collagen
proportionate area~(CPA) distributions across all six translation models, providing a
biologically grounded complement to patch-level and distributional metrics.

\subsection{Epistemic Uncertainty in Virtual Staining and I2I Translation}

Epistemic uncertainty---the component of predictive uncertainty attributable to limited
training data or model capacity---is most commonly quantified via Monte Carlo~(MC)
dropout~\cite{gal2016dropout}, which approximates Bayesian posterior sampling through
repeated stochastic forward passes at inference time, or via deep
ensembles~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple}, which measure disagreement across
independently trained models.
In digital histopathology, both approaches have been applied to classification and
segmentation on H\&E slides: uncertainty-informed models have been shown to enable
high-confidence predictions and automatic rejection of ambiguous
cases~\cite{uncertainty_informed_hist2022}.
In the specific context of collagen quantification, a U-Net ensemble trained on
Picrosirius Red liver biopsies from 686 cases across more than twenty clinical sites
produced epistemic uncertainty maps that reliably flagged artefacts and
out-of-distribution regions~\cite{liverfibrosis2025}, demonstrating that ensemble
uncertainty is a practical quality-control signal for SR-based analysis.

Within the I2I translation literature, uncertainty-awareness in the \textit{unpaired}
setting remains substantially underexplored.
Upadhyay~\etal~\cite{ugac2021} introduced an uncertainty-aware CycleGAN~(UGAC) that
replaces the standard $\ell_1$ cycle-consistency with a generalised Gaussian loss,
producing per-pixel epistemic uncertainty estimates that correlate with prediction error
and improve robustness to unseen test-time perturbations in MRI modality translation.
A systematic calibration study~\cite{trustworthy_i2i2025} subsequently evaluated
MC~dropout applied to CycleGAN and a diffusion-based model for unpaired mammography
translation, finding that standard MC~dropout is poorly calibrated in this regime and
proposing dedicated protocols for assessing trustworthiness when ground-truth pairs are
unavailable; poor MC~dropout calibration has been independently confirmed in biomedical
instance segmentation, where deep ensembles achieve well-calibrated estimates with
substantially less hyperparameter tuning~\cite{siddiqui2024uncertainty}.
In the closely related domain of virtual fluorescence staining of cultured cells,
ensemble-based image translation has been shown to produce uncertainty estimates that
predict per-tile translation error with a Pearson correlation of $r{\approx}0.83$ and
identify non-cellular artefacts as high-uncertainty regions~\cite{insilicolabeling2023}.

To our knowledge, no prior study has applied MC~dropout or deep ensembles to quantify
epistemic uncertainty in unpaired GAN- or diffusion-based \textit{histological stain}
translation, leaving unanswered which image regions are most uncertain and whether
uncertainty correlates with staining fidelity.
We address this gap by computing ensemble-based epistemic uncertainty maps for all six
models and comparing them against target-stain segmentation error, providing the first
systematic uncertainty analysis for unpaired virtual histological staining.
