\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Virtual Staining in Histopathology.}
Histopathological diagnosis relies on a panel of stains applied to thin tissue sections,
each requiring dedicated reagents, skilled technicians, and additional biopsy material.
Virtual staining uses deep learning to predict a target stain from an existing one,
offering a pathway to reduce tissue consumption, laboratory costs, and turn-around time
while standardising stain quality across centres~\cite{rivenson2020emerging, latonen2024virtual}.
In liver pathology, Sirius Red~(SR) staining is the clinical reference for collagen
quantification and fibrosis staging; obtaining it requires a separate section from the
same biopsy as the routinely acquired haematoxylin and eosin~(H\&E) slide.
Early virtual staining approaches relied on supervised pixel-to-pixel translation between
registered image pairs~\cite{isola2017image}, but obtaining geometrically aligned adjacent
sections is impractical at scale due to tissue deformation and processing artefacts,
motivating the shift towards unsupervised, unpaired learning
frameworks~\cite{aatresh2024virtual, chen2025challenges}.

\paragraph{Unsupervised Image-to-Image Translation.}
CycleGAN~\cite{zhu2017unpaired} established the dominant paradigm for unpaired translation
via cycle-consistent adversarial training.
Subsequent methods diversified the framework: UNIT~\cite{liu2017unsupervised} couples
domains through a shared VAE bottleneck; MUNIT~\cite{huang2018multimodal} disentangles
content from style via AdaIN for multimodal generation; DCLGAN~\cite{han2021dual} adds a
dual contrastive patch objective; and UVCGAN~\cite{torbunov2022uvcgan} replaces the ResNet
generator with a UNet--ViT hybrid augmented by masked-image pretraining.
CycleDiffusion~\cite{wu2022cyclediffusion} departs from the GAN paradigm by enforcing
cycle consistency through a shared diffusion noise space, encoding source images via DDIM
inversion and decoding into the target domain via DDIM sampling.

\paragraph{Applications to Histopathology Stain Translation.}
In liver histopathology specifically, an unsupervised GAN validated on $n{=}574$ NASH
WSIs~\cite{kieffer2020largescale} achieved a Turing-test pass rate among pathologists for
H\&E~$\to$~trichrome translation, and a prior-guided GAN~\cite{li2023unpaired} reached a
Spearman correlation of $\rho{=}0.82$ between virtual and real fibrosis scores for the
same task.
Recent benchmarks covering a broader set of models and stains~\cite{visgab2025,
staindiffuser2024, klockner2025he} consistently find that quantitative rankings disagree
with pathologist assessments, underscoring the need for task-based criteria.
Despite these advances, no study has directly compared the six model families evaluated
here on unpaired H\&E~$\to$~histochemical stain translation in mouse liver tissue.

\paragraph{Evaluation Frameworks for Virtual Staining.}
Evaluating virtual staining models poses unique challenges.
Dense pixel-level metrics such as PSNR require exact pixel correspondence and
co-registered ground-truth pairs, patch-based SSIM relaxes this requirement by
computing structural similarity over local windows rather than the full image, and
distributional metrics such as FID capture population-level realism without
any pairing.
Khan~\etal~\cite{khan2023effect} showed that standard image quality metrics do not always
correlate with histological feasibility in virtual H\&E staining, underscoring the need
for pathology-aware evaluation.
Kl\"{o}ckner~\etal~\cite{klockner2025he} benchmarked paired and unpaired models for
breast cancer IHC generation on the BCI and MIST datasets, reporting that quantitative
rankings frequently disagree with qualitative pathologist assessments.
The StainDiffuser benchmark~\cite{staindiffuser2024} is the most comprehensive to date,
evaluating more than twenty GAN-based and diffusion-based I2I models with a consistent
metric suite.
Our study contributes a complementary evaluation of Sirius Red, an underexplored
histochemical stain for collagen and fibrosis quantification, using mouse liver tissue
from a bile-duct ligation model of cholestatic liver disease~\cite{ghallab2025asbt},
assessing all six models with CPA MAE as the primary task-based benchmark,
complemented by perceptual and distributional measures.

A growing body of work argues for \textit{task-based evaluation}, in which downstream
clinical tasks are applied to virtual images to verify that diagnostically relevant
content is faithfully reproduced~\cite{renal_seg2025, her2task2024}.
For collagen quantification specifically, deep learning models achieve Dice scores of
$0.83$--$0.90$ on Sirius Red~\cite{liverfibrosis2025}, enabling automated CPA measurement
as a task-based quality criterion.
We adopt this paradigm by applying a frozen SR collagen segmentation model identically to
real and virtual SR images, computing CPA~MAE as the primary benchmark, complemented by
FID, LPIPS, and patch-based SSIM.

\paragraph{Epistemic Uncertainty in Virtual Staining and I2I Translation.}
Epistemic uncertainty, referring to the component of predictive uncertainty attributable
to limited training data or model capacity, is most commonly quantified via
Monte Carlo~(MC) dropout~\cite{gal2016dropout}, which approximates Bayesian posterior sampling through
repeated stochastic forward passes at inference time, or via deep
ensembles~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple}, which measure disagreement across
independently trained models.
In digital histopathology, both approaches have been applied to classification and
segmentation on H\&E slides: uncertainty-informed models have been shown to enable
high-confidence predictions and automatic rejection of ambiguous
cases~\cite{uncertainty_informed_hist2022}.
In the specific context of collagen quantification, a U-Net ensemble trained on
Picrosirius Red liver biopsies from 686 cases across more than twenty clinical sites
produced epistemic uncertainty maps that reliably flagged artefacts and
out-of-distribution regions~\cite{liverfibrosis2025}, demonstrating that ensemble
uncertainty is a practical quality-control signal for SR-based analysis.

Within the I2I translation literature, uncertainty-awareness in the \textit{unpaired}
setting remains underexplored.
UGAC~\cite{ugac2021} extended CycleGAN with a generalised Gaussian cycle-consistency loss
to produce per-pixel epistemic uncertainty estimates for MRI modality translation.
A calibration study~\cite{trustworthy_i2i2025} found MC~dropout poorly calibrated for
unpaired image translation, while deep ensembles yield well-calibrated estimates with
minimal tuning~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple, siddiqui2024uncertainty}.
In virtual cell staining, ensemble-based translation produced uncertainty estimates with
Pearson $r{\approx}0.83$ against per-tile translation error~\cite{insilicolabeling2023}.

To our knowledge, no prior study has applied MC~dropout or deep ensembles to quantify
epistemic uncertainty in unpaired GAN- or diffusion-based \textit{histological stain}
translation, leaving unanswered which image regions are most uncertain and whether
uncertainty correlates with staining fidelity.
We address this gap by computing ensemble-based epistemic uncertainty maps for all six
models and comparing them against target-stain segmentation error, providing the first
systematic uncertainty analysis for unpaired virtual histological staining.
