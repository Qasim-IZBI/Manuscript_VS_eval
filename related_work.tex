\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Virtual Staining in Histopathology:}
Virtual staining predicts a target stain from an existing one, conserving tissue and
turn-around time~\cite{rivenson2020emerging, latonen2024virtual}.
Early supervised methods required registered image pairs~\cite{isola2017image},
impractical to obtain at scale due to tissue deformation~\cite{aatresh2024virtual,
chen2025challenges}, motivating the shift to unsupervised, unpaired approaches.

\textbf{Unsupervised Image-to-Image Translation:}
This shift has produced a diverse family of unpaired I2I methods.
CycleGAN~\cite{zhu2017unpaired} established the dominant paradigm via cycle-consistent
adversarial training. Subsequent methods extended the framework:
UNIT~\cite{liu2017unsupervised} couples domains through a shared VAE bottleneck;
MUNIT~\cite{huang2018multimodal} disentangles content from style via AdaIN for multimodal
generation; DCLGAN~\cite{han2021dual} adds a dual contrastive patch objective; and
UVCGAN~\cite{torbunov2022uvcgan} replaces the ResNet generator with a
U-Net~\cite{ronneberger2015unet}--ViT~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit} hybrid pretrained with
masked-image modelling.
CycleDiffusion~\cite{wu2022cyclediffusion} departs from the GAN paradigm: cycle
consistency is enforced in a shared diffusion noise space, with DDIM~\cite{song2021ddim}
inversion encoding source images and DDIM sampling decoding them in the target domain.

\textbf{Applications to Histopathology Stain Translation:}
In liver histopathology specifically, an unsupervised GAN validated on $n{=}574$ NASH
WSIs~\cite{kieffer2020largescale} passed a pathologist Turing test for
H\&E~$\to$~trichrome translation, and a prior-guided GAN~\cite{li2023unpaired} reached a
Spearman correlation of $r_s{=}0.82$ between virtual and real fibrosis scores on the same
task.
Recent benchmarks covering a broader set of models and stains~\cite{visgab2025,
staindiffuser2024, klockner2025he} consistently find that quantitative rankings disagree
with pathologist assessments, underscoring the need for task-specific criteria.
Despite these advances, no prior study has compared the six model families evaluated here
on unpaired H\&E~$\to$~histochemical stain translation in mouse liver tissue.

\textbf{Evaluation Frameworks for Virtual Staining:}
Evaluating virtual staining models poses unique challenges. Pixel-level metrics such as
the structural similarity index measure~(SSIM)~\cite{wang2004ssim} require co-registered
references that are unavailable in the unpaired setting; perceptual metrics such as the
learned perceptual image patch similarity~(LPIPS)~\cite{zhang2018lpips} compare
deep-feature embeddings without strict pairing; and distributional metrics such as the
Fr\'echet Inception Distance~(FID)~\cite{heusel2017fid} capture population-level realism.
Khan~\etal~\cite{khan2023effect} showed that standard image quality metrics do not always
reflect histological feasibility in virtual H\&E staining, motivating pathology-aware
evaluation.
A growing body of work therefore argues for \textit{task-specific evaluation}: applying
downstream clinical tasks to virtual images to verify that diagnostically relevant
content is preserved~\cite{renal_seg2025, her2task2024}.
For collagen quantification specifically, deep learning segmentation models reach Dice
scores of $0.83$--$0.90$ on Sirius Red~\cite{liverfibrosis2025}, demonstrating that
automated CPA measurement is clinically viable.
We adopt this paradigm via a frozen SR collagen segmentation model applied identically
to real and virtual SR images, reporting CPA~MAE as the primary task-specific benchmark.

\textbf{Epistemic Uncertainty in Virtual Staining and I2I Translation:}
Epistemic uncertainty captures the component of predictive uncertainty attributable to
limited training data or model capacity. It is most commonly quantified via Monte
Carlo~(MC) dropout~\cite{gal2016dropout}, which approximates Bayesian posterior sampling
through repeated stochastic forward passes, or via deep
ensembles~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple}, which measure disagreement across
independently trained models.
In digital histopathology, both approaches have been applied to H\&E classification and
segmentation, enabling high-confidence predictions and automatic rejection of ambiguous
cases~\cite{uncertainty_informed_hist2022}.
Yet within the I2I translation literature, uncertainty-awareness in the \textit{unpaired}
setting remains underexplored.
UGAC~\cite{ugac2021} extended CycleGAN with a generalised Gaussian cycle-consistency loss
to produce per-pixel epistemic uncertainty estimates for MRI modality translation.
A calibration study~\cite{trustworthy_i2i2025} found MC~dropout poorly calibrated for
unpaired image translation, while deep ensembles yield well-calibrated estimates with
minimal tuning~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple, siddiqui2024uncertainty}.
In virtual cell staining, ensemble-based translation produced uncertainty estimates with
Pearson $\rho{\approx}0.83$ against per-tile translation error~\cite{insilicolabeling2023}.

To our knowledge, no prior study has applied MC~dropout or deep ensembles to quantify
epistemic uncertainty in unpaired GAN- or diffusion-based \textit{histological stain}
translation, leaving open which image regions are most uncertain and how informative the
resulting ensemble disagreement is in practice.
We address this gap by computing ensemble-based epistemic uncertainty maps for the best
configuration per family, summarised as per-family mean ensemble variance, providing the
first systematic comparison of epistemic uncertainty across unsupervised
stain-to-stain architectures.
