\section{Introduction}

Histopathological diagnosis relies on a panel of specialised stains, each designed to
highlight a distinct tissue component: haematoxylin and eosin~(H\&E) provides general
morphological context, while dedicated stains target specific biological questions by
visualising collagen fibres, proliferating cells, or receptor-expressing
cells~\cite{lefkowitch2006special, krishna2013role}.
In clinical and preclinical practice, each additional stain requires a separate tissue
section, consuming limited biopsy material~\cite{rivenson2020emerging, dehaan2021deep},
extending laboratory processing time~\cite{dehaan2021deep}, and introducing inevitable
spatial misalignment between staining layers due to tissue deformation during sectioning
and mounting~\cite{gatenbee2023virtual, chen2022mvfstain}.
This multiplies with every marker of interest: a comprehensive tissue characterisation
panel may require four or more serial sections from a single biopsy~\cite{clark2017immunohistochemistry},
placing practical limits on the breadth of analysis achievable from any given specimen.

In liver pathology, Sirius Red~(SR) staining is of particular importance.
SR binds collagen fibres with high specificity and is the histochemical gold standard
for collagen proportionate area~(CPA) measurement and fibrosis staging, correlating more
strongly with serum fibrosis markers than Masson's trichrome~\cite{huang2013sirius}.
In practice, obtaining SR requires a separate tissue section from the same biopsy block,
consuming limited material and introducing spatial misalignment between the SR and H\&E
slides due to tissue deformation during sectioning.

Virtual staining, which uses deep learning to predict a target stain directly from an
existing digitised slide, offers an attractive solution.
By eliminating the need for additional sections, it conserves tissue,
reduces turn-around time, and creates the possibility of retrospective stain analysis from
H\&E archives~\cite{rivenson2020emerging, latonen2024virtual}.
The field has advanced rapidly: supervised pixel-to-pixel translation models have been
validated for H\&E~$\to$~trichrome on large liver cohorts~\cite{kieffer2020largescale},
and a prior-guided GAN has achieved a Spearman correlation of $\rho{=}0.82$ between
virtual and real trichrome-based fibrosis scores~\cite{li2023unpaired}.
Yet supervised approaches require geometrically registered image pairs from adjacent
sections, a requirement that is impractical at scale and introduces systematic bias from
tissue deformation~\cite{aatresh2024virtual}.
\textit{Unsupervised} methods, which learn domain mappings from unpaired collections, are
therefore better suited to this setting. However, no study has directly applied them to
histochemical stain translation in liver tissue, and it remains unclear which model
within the increasingly diverse family of unpaired image-to-image~(I2I) approaches
performs best for this task.

Since the introduction of cycle-consistent adversarial networks~(CycleGAN;~\cite{zhu2017unpaired}),
the unpaired I2I field has diversified considerably.
UNIT~\cite{liu2017unsupervised} couples domain translation via a shared VAE latent space;
MUNIT~\cite{huang2018multimodal} disentangles content from style to enable multimodal
generation; DCLGAN~\cite{han2021dual} adds a dual contrastive objective to improve
patch-level correspondence; UVCGAN~\cite{torbunov2022uvcgan} replaces the convolutional
generator with a UNet--Vision Transformer hybrid; and CycleDiffusion~\cite{wu2022cyclediffusion} enforces cycle consistency through a
shared diffusion noise space via DDIM inversion and sampling.
These architectures differ substantially in capacity, inductive bias, and data requirements,
yet comparative studies have focused almost exclusively on H\&E~$\to$~IHC or skin tissue
staining~\cite{visgab2025, adib2024dual}, leaving their relative suitability for
histochemical stain translation in liver tissue uncharacterised.
Moreover, existing benchmarks typically fix a single model scale and data budget,
providing no insight into how model capacity and training set size jointly determine
translation quality, a practically important question given that liver biopsy cohorts
vary enormously in size across clinical centres.

Reliable deployment of virtual staining also requires an understanding of predictive
confidence.
Epistemic uncertainty, the component of model uncertainty reducible with more
data, can be estimated via Monte Carlo~(MC) dropout or deep
ensembles~\cite{gal2016dropout, lakshminarayanan2017simple}, and both techniques have been
applied to H\&E-based classification and SR collagen segmentation~\cite{uncertainty_informed_hist2022, liverfibrosis2025}.
Yet uncertainty quantification has not been applied to unpaired I2I translation for
histological staining: existing uncertainty-aware cycle-consistency work targets MRI
modality translation~\cite{ugac2021}, and a recent calibration study found standard
MC~dropout poorly suited to unpaired generation~\cite{trustworthy_i2i2025}, motivating
the use of deep ensembles.
Without spatially resolved uncertainty maps, a pathologist has no means to identify
which regions of a virtually stained slide should be treated with caution, a critical
gap for clinical adoption.

To address these open questions, this paper makes four contributions.

First, we introduce and openly publish a whole-slide image dataset of mouse liver tissue
sections from a bile-duct ligation~(BDL) model of cholestatic liver disease~\cite{ghallab2025asbt},
comprising paired H\&E and Sirius Red~(SR) stains, providing the first dedicated resource
for unsupervised H\&E~$\to$~SR translation research in mouse liver tissue.

Building on this dataset, we conduct a systematic scaling study in which CycleGAN, UNIT,
MUNIT, DCLGAN, UVCGAN, and CycleDiffusion are each trained at three generator capacities
(${\sim}10$M, ${\sim}50$M, and ${\sim}100$M parameters) and on three data fractions
(25\%, 50\%, and 100\% of the training tiles), yielding 54 experimental configurations.
This design directly characterises how model capacity and data volume jointly govern
stain translation quality, an aspect not addressed by prior benchmarks.

To evaluate all 54 configurations rigorously, we combine perceptual and task-based
measures. Because adjacent-section pairs are only loosely registered, we report
patch-based SSIM alongside FID and LPIPS as distributional and perceptual measures.
We further apply a collagen segmentation model to both real and virtual SR images and
compute the mean absolute error~(MAE) of paired collagen proportionate area~(CPA)
values, providing a biologically grounded criterion that exploits the paired structure
of our dataset.

Finally, from the top-performing configuration per model family we train deep ensembles
of ten independently initialised models and compute spatially resolved epistemic
uncertainty maps. To our knowledge, this constitutes the first systematic uncertainty
analysis for unpaired virtual histological staining, and reveals how predictive
confidence correlates with target-stain segmentation fidelity across model families.
Together, these contributions establish a reproducible framework, including an open
dataset and public benchmark, for future work on virtual histochemical staining and
uncertainty-aware virtual histopathology.
