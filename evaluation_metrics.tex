\section{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

Evaluating virtual staining presents a fundamental measurement challenge: H\&E and
Sirius Red images are obtained from \textit{adjacent} tissue sections rather than the
same physical section, so even a geometrically perfect translation cannot produce a
pixel-identical match to the reference owing to tissue deformation, differences in
section thickness, and staining variability between sections.
We therefore combine three complementary families of metrics --- distributional,
perceptual, and task-based --- each of which is sensitive to a different aspect of
translation quality and carries different assumptions about correspondence between
real and virtual images.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Fréchet Inception Distance}
\label{sec:fid}
% -------------------------------------------------------

The Fréchet Inception Distance~(FID;~\cite{heusel2017fid}) measures the divergence
between the distribution of real SR tiles and the distribution of virtually generated
SR tiles without requiring any pairing between individual images.
Feature representations are extracted from the penultimate layer of an
InceptionV3~\cite{szegedy2016inception} network pre-trained on ImageNet, and both sets
of features are modelled as multivariate Gaussians with means $\mu_r$, $\mu_g$ and
covariances $\Sigma_r$, $\Sigma_g$.
The FID is then:
\begin{equation}
  \text{FID} = \|\mu_r - \mu_g\|_2^2 +
    \operatorname{tr}\!\bigl(\Sigma_r + \Sigma_g -
      2(\Sigma_r \Sigma_g)^{1/2}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Lower FID indicates that the generated distribution is statistically closer to the real
SR distribution.
Because FID operates at the population level it is insensitive to misalignment between
individual tile pairs, making it the primary distributional quality measure under our
loosely registered dataset.
FID is computed over the full test set; a minimum of 2{,}048 images per split is used
to obtain stable estimates~\cite{heusel2017fid}.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity}
\label{sec:lpips}
% -------------------------------------------------------

LPIPS~\cite{zhang2018lpips} measures perceptual distance between two images by
comparing their deep feature representations across multiple layers of a
VGG16~\cite{simonyan2015vgg} network.
For each tile pair $(x_{\text{real}},\, x_{\text{virtual}})$ matched by tile
identifier from adjacent sections, activations at multiple network depths are
normalised channel-wise and their $\ell_2$ distances are summed with learned scalar
weights:
\begin{equation}
  \text{LPIPS}(x_r, x_g) = \sum_l w_l \,
    \bigl\| \hat{\phi}_l(x_r) - \hat{\phi}_l(x_g) \bigr\|_2^2,
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\phi}_l$ denotes unit-normalised activations at layer $l$ and $w_l$ are
weights optimised to match human perceptual judgements~\cite{zhang2018lpips}.
Lower LPIPS reflects greater perceptual similarity.
Although LPIPS uses paired tiles, the metric is more tolerant of the mild spatial
misalignment present in adjacent-section pairs than pixel-level measures, because it
operates on spatially pooled feature maps rather than raw pixel intensities.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Patch-Based Structural Similarity Index}
\label{sec:ssim}
% -------------------------------------------------------

The Structural Similarity Index~(SSIM;~\cite{wang2004ssim}) quantifies luminance,
contrast, and structural agreement between two images within local windows.
Full-image SSIM is unreliable under loose inter-section registration because even
sub-pixel tissue shifts depress the score due to its sensitivity to exact spatial
correspondence.
We therefore adopt a \textit{patch-based} variant: for each tile pair, $N_p$ patches of
size $p{\times}p$ pixels are randomly sampled from non-overlapping spatial locations,
SSIM is computed within each patch independently, and the per-tile score is reported as
the mean across patches.
This strategy reduces the contribution of global misalignment to the final score while
preserving sensitivity to local textural and structural differences that are
diagnostically relevant for collagen fibre assessment.
Patch size and count are specified in Section~\ref{sec:exp_setup}.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Task-Based Evaluation: Positive-Area Agreement}
\label{sec:task_eval}
% -------------------------------------------------------

Pixel-level and perceptual metrics assess visual similarity to a reference image
but do not directly measure whether a virtually stained slide supports the same
biological conclusions as the real stain.
We therefore complement the above measures with a task-based evaluation that tests
whether the virtual stain output faithfully preserves the positive-stained area of
the target marker.

\paragraph{Sirius Red (primary evaluation).}
A collagen segmentation model~(see Section~\ref{sec:sr_seg_model}) trained exclusively
on real SR tiles is applied identically to (i)~the real SR test tiles and (ii)~the
virtual SR tiles produced by each model configuration.
For each tile, the segmentation yields a binary collagen mask from which the
\textit{collagen proportionate area}~(CPA) --- the fraction of tissue area occupied by
Sirius Red-positive collagen --- is computed.
CPA is the standard quantitative readout for hepatic fibrosis assessment and is
directly comparable across slides~\cite{huang2013sirius}.

Distributional agreement between real and virtual CPA is quantified at the WSI level.
For each condition, one CPA value per WSI is derived by dividing the total
PSR-labelled pixels by all tissue-labelled pixels across the reconstructed WSI
(background excluded).
The primary metric is the \textit{Wasserstein-1 distance}~(earth-mover distance)
between the real-SR CPA distribution and each model's virtual-SR CPA distribution,
which measures the minimum transport cost between distributions and is robust to
differences in distribution shape~\cite{villani2009optimal}.
Uncertainty in the Wasserstein estimate is reported as a 95\% bootstrap confidence
interval constructed by WSI-level resampling (1{,}000 iterations), providing
honest uncertainty quantification given the small test-set size
($n{=}5$~WSIs; see Section~\ref{sec:tiling}).
As a supplementary directional measure, the signed mean difference
(virtual\,$-$\,real) and the standard-deviation ratio (virtual\,/\,real) are reported
to characterise the bias and spread of each model's CPA output.
The Kolmogorov--Smirnov~(KS) statistic is included for completeness; owing to the
limited sample size, it is interpreted as directional evidence only and not used as a
decision criterion.
Lower Wasserstein-1 distance indicates that the virtual stain supports collagen
quantification consistent with the real stain, providing a biologically grounded
evaluation criterion that perceptual metrics cannot capture.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Task-Based Evaluation: Cross-Stain Spatial Consistency}
\label{sec:cross_stain}
% -------------------------------------------------------

The positive-area agreement metric~(Section~\ref{sec:task_eval}) evaluates whether
the total collagen fraction is preserved across the distribution of test WSIs, but
does not assess whether PSR-positive signal is generated in spatially correct
locations within a tile.
We therefore introduce a complementary spatial fidelity measure that exploits the
pixel-level alignment between each H\&E input tile and its corresponding generated
SR output tile.

An acellular eosinophilic region mask --- serving as a collagen proxy --- is
extracted from each H\&E input tile using Macenko colour
deconvolution~\cite{[TODO: macenko2009]} followed by nuclear exclusion: the eosin
channel response above a fixed threshold is masked by a dilated haematoxylin mask
to remove nuclear and cytoplasmic signal, and connected components below a minimum
area are discarded.
The resulting mask represents acellular eosinophilic extracellular matrix~(ECM),
which is a tissue-level collagen proxy in fibrotic liver.
This collagen proxy mask is compared against the PSR-positive region mask produced
by the frozen nnU-Net~v2 segmentation model~(Section~\ref{sec:sr_seg_model})
applied to the corresponding generated SR tile.
Because each H\&E tile and its generated SR counterpart share identical spatial
coordinates by construction, no registration is required, and this evaluation is
unaffected by the inter-section misalignment that complicates the paired metrics
in Sections~\ref{sec:lpips} and~\ref{sec:ssim}.

Spatial agreement is quantified by the Dice similarity coefficient~(DSC) and
Intersection-over-Union~(IoU), reported as mean~$\pm$~standard deviation across
all test WSIs.
A model that generates PSR signal preferentially in acellular eosinophilic regions
will achieve high DSC even if its total CPA differs from real SR; conversely, a
model that distributes PSR signal independently of the H\&E collagen structure will
score poorly.
This evaluation requires no real SR images and is therefore independent of the
CPA distribution comparison, constituting a complementary signal of spatial
translation fidelity.

\textit{Limitation.}
The eosin channel cannot cleanly separate collagen from other eosinophilic ECM
components~(fibrin, basement membrane).
The collagen proxy represents acellular eosinophilic ECM rather than collagen
specifically; however, any resulting imprecision is applied identically across all
model conditions, so relative DSC comparisons between models remain valid.

\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:metrics_summary}
% -------------------------------------------------------

Table~\ref{tab:metrics_summary} summarises the five metrics, their pairing
requirements, and the aspect of translation quality each addresses.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Overview of evaluation metrics. ``Paired'' indicates whether the metric
requires tile-level correspondence between real and virtual SR images.}
\label{tab:metrics_summary}
\begin{tabular}{lccl}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Paired} & \textbf{Lower is better} &
\textbf{Aspect assessed} \\
\midrule
FID                        & No  & \checkmark & Population-level distributional similarity \\
LPIPS                      & Yes & \checkmark & Deep perceptual similarity \\
Patch SSIM                 & Yes & ---        & Local structural and textural similarity \\
CPA agreement (Wasserstein-1) & No  & \checkmark & Collagen proportionate area distribution fidelity \\

\end{tabular}
\end{table}
