# Related Work

## Virtual Staining in Histopathology

Histopathological diagnosis relies on a panel of stains applied to thin tissue sections, each requiring dedicated reagents, skilled technicians, and additional biopsy material. Virtual staining — the computational prediction of a target stain from an existing one using deep learning — offers a pathway to reduce tissue consumption, laboratory costs, and turn-around time while standardising stain quality across centres [Rivenson et al., 2020; Latonen et al., 2024]. In liver pathology, Sirius Red (SR) staining is the clinical reference for collagen quantification and fibrosis staging; obtaining it requires a separate section from the same biopsy as the routinely acquired haematoxylin and eosin (H&E) slide. Early virtual staining approaches relied on supervised pixel-to-pixel translation between registered image pairs [Isola et al., 2017], but obtaining geometrically aligned adjacent sections is impractical at scale due to tissue deformation and processing artefacts, motivating the shift towards unsupervised, unpaired learning frameworks [systematic review, BMC Medical Imaging, 2024; Challenges & perspectives, 2025].

## Unsupervised Image-to-Image Translation

Cycle-consistent adversarial networks (CycleGAN; Zhu et al., 2017) established the dominant paradigm for unpaired stain translation by training two generators with a cycle-consistency loss that enforces invertibility without pixel-level supervision. UNIT (Liu et al., 2017) extended this idea with a shared VAE bottleneck, encouraging both domains to occupy a common latent space and enabling more coherent cross-domain mappings. MUNIT (Huang et al., 2018) introduced a content–style disentanglement, separating domain-invariant spatial structure from domain-specific appearance via Adaptive Instance Normalisation, enabling multimodal generation from a single source image. DCLGAN (Han et al., 2021) augmented the CycleGAN framework with a dual contrastive objective that maximises mutual information between corresponding patches in both translation directions, improving embedding quality without increasing generator complexity. UVCGAN (Torbunov et al., 2022) revisited cycle-consistent training with a UNet–Vision Transformer hybrid generator and self-supervised masked image pretraining, achieving stronger preservation of long-range structural correlations. More recently, score-based diffusion models have been applied to this problem: Liu et al. (2025) proposed a mutual-information-guided reverse diffusion process with timestep-customised noise scheduling and a local patch-contrastive loss for unpaired H&E → IHC translation, offering finer control over staining intensity and structural reconstruction than adversarial methods alone.

## Applications to Histopathology Stain Translation

These architectures have been adopted for a growing range of histopathological stain conversion tasks. CycleGAN remains the most widely deployed backbone: Liu et al. (2021) reported the first virtual Ki-67 IHC staining from H&E in breast cancer, incorporating a pathological representation network to penalise deviations in tumour-cell spatial predictions between the forward and backward generators. MVFStain (2022) drew on MUNIT's style–content separation to train a single network producing nine IHC and functional stains from H&E across lung and breast tissue. In liver histopathology, an early large-scale validation study ([TBC] et al., 2020) demonstrated that an unsupervised GAN could translate H&E to Masson's trichrome on n=574 NASH whole-slide images (WSIs), with pathologists unable to distinguish virtual from chemically stained slides in a Turing test. A subsequent prior-guided GAN (PG-GAN; [TBC] et al., 2023) embedded pathology-feature constraints directly into the generator for H&E → trichrome translation on liver biopsy WSIs, achieving a Spearman correlation of ρ=0.82 between virtual and real stain fibrosis scores. DCLGAN was applied to a bespoke virtual staining task by Adib et al. (2024), translating autofluorescence images of unstained skin tissue to H&E with 90.2% diagnostic concordance among dermatopathologists. UVCGAN has been included as a strong unpaired baseline in multi-domain staining studies, though recent comparisons for H&E → fibre-stain translation (ViFIT; 2025) found it struggled to generate valid fibre texture. Despite these advances, no study to date has directly compared these six model families on unpaired H&E → Sirius Red translation in liver tissue.

## Evaluation Frameworks for Virtual Staining

Evaluating virtual staining models poses unique challenges. Pixel-level metrics (SSIM, PSNR, MAE) require co-registered ground-truth pairs, while distributional metrics such as FID and KID measure population-level realism without assessing spatial correspondence. Khan et al. (2023) showed that standard image quality metrics do not always correlate with histological feasibility in virtual H&E staining, underscoring the need for pathology-aware evaluation. VISGAB (2025) introduced a histology-specific fidelity index (HSFI) — derived from pathologist concordance experiments on skin tissue — and found that CycleGAN outperforms DCLGAN and CUTGAN on structural fidelity despite slower inference. Klöckner et al. (2025) benchmarked paired and unpaired models for breast cancer IHC generation on the BCI and MIST datasets, reporting that quantitative rankings frequently disagree with qualitative pathologist assessments. The StainDiffuser benchmark (2024) is the most comprehensive to date, evaluating more than twenty GAN-based and diffusion-based I2I models with a consistent metric suite. Our study contributes a complementary evaluation focused on an underexplored collagen stain target — Sirius Red in liver tissue — using a dataset collected in-house, and assesses all six models using both pixel-level and distributional metrics alongside qualitative expert review.

A growing body of work argues that image quality metrics alone are insufficient and advocates for *task-based evaluation*, in which downstream clinical tasks are applied to virtually generated images to test whether diagnostically relevant content is faithfully reproduced. This paradigm has been applied in several staining contexts. In renal histopathology, a recent study used CycleGAN-based H&E → PAS virtual staining as a prerequisite for glomerular segmentation across multiple centres [Virtual Histological Staining for Renal Segmentation, 2025]: segmentation models trained on real PAS slides were applied to virtually stained test images, and the resulting glomerular detection performance served as the primary quality criterion. In the IHC domain, task-based evaluation has been formalised for HER2 scoring: virtual H&E → HER2 IHC images were assessed not by pixel similarity but by whether a downstream HER2 classifier produced concordant scores with those obtained on real IHC [Advancing H&E-to-IHC with Task-Specific Domain Knowledge, MICCAI 2024]. For collagen quantification specifically, deep learning models have been developed to segment positive Sirius Red areas in liver WSIs and score fibrosis severity [Fibrosis severity scoring on Sirius Red, Biological Imaging 2024], and U-Net-based collagen segmentation on Sirius Red achieves Dice scores of 0.83–0.90 across heterogeneous staining conditions [Enhancing liver fibrosis measurement, 2025]. We adopt this task-based paradigm by applying a Sirius Red collagen segmentation model to both real and virtually generated SR images and comparing the resulting positive-area distributions across all six translation models, providing a clinically grounded complement to pixel-level and distributional metrics.

## Epistemic Uncertainty in Virtual Staining and I2I Translation

Epistemic uncertainty — the component of predictive uncertainty attributable to limited training data or model capacity — is most commonly quantified via Monte Carlo (MC) dropout [Gal & Ghahramani, 2016], which approximates Bayesian posterior sampling through repeated stochastic forward passes at inference time, or via deep ensembles [Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017], which measure disagreement across independently trained models. In digital histopathology, both approaches have been applied to classification and segmentation on H&E slides: uncertainty-informed models enable high-confidence predictions and automatic rejection of ambiguous cases [Ghoshal & Tucker, 2022]. In the specific context of collagen quantification, a U-Net ensemble trained on Picrosirius Red liver biopsies from 686 cases across more than twenty clinical sites produced epistemic uncertainty maps that reliably flagged artefacts and out-of-distribution regions [Enhancing liver fibrosis measurement, 2025], demonstrating that ensemble uncertainty is a practical quality-control signal for SR-based analysis.

Within the I2I translation literature, uncertainty-awareness in the *unpaired* setting remains substantially underexplored. Upadhyay et al. (NeurIPS 2021) introduced an uncertainty-aware CycleGAN (UGAC) that replaces the standard ℓ₁ cycle-consistency with a generalised Gaussian loss, producing per-pixel epistemic uncertainty estimates that correlate with prediction error and improve robustness to unseen test-time perturbations in MRI modality translation. A systematic calibration study [arXiv:2501.17570, 2025] subsequently evaluated MC dropout applied to CycleGAN and a diffusion-based model for unpaired mammography translation, finding that standard MC dropout is poorly calibrated in this regime and proposing dedicated protocols for assessing trustworthiness when ground-truth pairs are unavailable. In the closely related domain of virtual fluorescence staining of cultured cells, ensemble-based image translation has been shown to produce uncertainty estimates that predict per-tile translation error with a Pearson correlation of r ≈ 0.83 and identify non-cellular artefacts as high-uncertainty regions [Trustworthy in silico cell labeling, 2023].

To our knowledge, no prior study has applied MC dropout or deep ensembles to quantify epistemic uncertainty in unpaired GAN- or diffusion-based *histological stain* translation, leaving unanswered which image regions are most uncertain and whether uncertainty correlates with staining fidelity. We address this gap by computing ensemble-based epistemic uncertainty maps for all six models and comparing them against the SR segmentation error, providing the first systematic uncertainty analysis for unpaired virtual histological staining.
