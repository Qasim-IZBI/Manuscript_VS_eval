\subsection{Results: Scaling Study}
\label{sec:results_scaling}

Results are reported for three model families (CycleGAN, UNIT, MUNIT) across all nine
scaling configurations ($3~\text{sizes} \times 3~\text{data fractions}$).
FID, LPIPS, and patch-based SSIM are computed once over the full test tile set and carry
no per-sample variance estimate; CPA MAE is computed per WSI and reported as
mean~$\pm$~std over $N{=}5$ held-out test WSIs.
Figure~\ref{fig:results_overview} summarises all four metrics across the 27~configurations.

\subsubsection{Effect of Model Size}
\label{sec:results_size}

Referring to Figure~\ref{fig:results_overview}, we examine how each metric responds to
increasing generator capacity within each model family.

\paragraph{CycleGAN.}
At 25\% and 50\% training data, FID and CPA MAE are relatively stable across model
sizes (FID: 109.0–118.4 and 107.2–138.6 respectively; MAE: 0.045–0.053 and 0.055–0.063),
with no consistent directional trend.
At full data, the medium model achieves the lowest FID of any CycleGAN configuration
(103.7) and the lowest CPA MAE overall ($0.008~{\pm}~0.007$, Spearman $\rho{=}1.0$,
Pearson $r{=}0.997$).
The large model at full data produces the highest CPA MAE of any configuration tested
($0.085~{\pm}~0.032$), despite the small and medium models both performing well at the
same data fraction.
LPIPS ($0.0476$–$0.0480$) and patch-based SSIM ($0.00119$–$0.00133$) are
effectively constant across all CycleGAN size configurations.

\paragraph{UNIT.}
FID decreases with model size at 25\% data (small: 183.7, medium: 179.6, large: 172.9)
and the large model achieves the lowest FID at full data (170.8 vs.\ 190.0 for medium
and 187.2 for small), giving a consistent directional trend across data fractions.
CPA MAE also favours the large model at 25\% and 50\% data (large/small: $0.024~{\pm}~0.041$;
large/medium: $0.023~{\pm}~0.030$), while at full data all three sizes produce
comparable MAE values in the range $0.037$–$0.046$.
LPIPS ($0.0532$–$0.0535$) and patch-based SSIM are flat across all UNIT size configurations.

\paragraph{MUNIT.}
Neither FID nor CPA MAE shows a consistent directional response to model size for MUNIT.
FID ranges from 163.7 to 210.0 with no monotonic trend within any data fraction.
CPA MAE values lie in the range $0.039$–$0.059$ across all configurations, with no model
size consistently outperforming the others across data fractions.
LPIPS ($0.0536$–$0.0541$) and patch-based SSIM remain flat.

\subsubsection{Effect of Training Data Size}
\label{sec:results_data}

Referring again to Figure~\ref{fig:results_overview}, we examine how each metric responds
to increasing training data fraction within each model family.

\paragraph{CycleGAN.}
For the medium model, FID decreases from 138.6 at 50\% data to 103.7 at full data, the
largest single-step FID improvement observed across all CycleGAN configurations.
CPA MAE for the medium model drops sharply from $0.057$ at 50\% to $0.008$ at full data,
while the small model MAE remains in the range $0.045$–$0.063$ across all data fractions
with no clear trend.
The large model shows the opposite pattern: MAE at full data ($0.085~{\pm}~0.032$) is
substantially worse than at 25\% ($0.052~{\pm}~0.062$) or 50\% ($0.055~{\pm}~0.061$).

\paragraph{UNIT.}
FID shows no consistent improvement with additional training data for UNIT; values
distribute in the range 170.4–190.0 with no directional trend within any model size.
CPA MAE is lowest at 50\% data for both the small model (small/medium: $0.017~{\pm}~0.020$)
and the large model (large/medium: $0.023~{\pm}~0.030$).
At full data, MAE values for the small and large models are higher than at 50\%, while
the medium model shows a modest improvement from 50\% ($0.047$) to full data ($0.037$).
Additional training data beyond 50\% does not consistently reduce CPA MAE for UNIT.

\paragraph{MUNIT.}
FID increases with data fraction for MUNIT: the two highest FID values across all
configurations are both at full data (small/large: 209.0; large/large: 210.0), compared
to a minimum of 163.7 at 25\% data (medium/small).
This represents a consistent degradation in distributional realism with additional
training data, a pattern not observed in either CycleGAN or UNIT.
CPA MAE remains within a narrow band ($0.041$–$0.059$) across all data fractions and
model sizes, with no configuration benefiting consistently from more training data.

\subsubsection{Metric Observations}
\label{sec:results_metric_agreement}

LPIPS and patch-based SSIM show negligible variation across all 27~configurations
(LPIPS: $0.0476$–$0.0541$; patch-SSIM: below $0.002$ throughout), indicating that
these metrics do not distinguish between model families or scaling configurations at the
ranges explored here.
FID provides separation at the level of model families — CycleGAN configurations
(103.7–138.6) score consistently lower than UNIT (170.4–190.0) and MUNIT
(163.7–210.0) — reflecting distributional differences in the generated stain
appearance across architectures.
CPA MAE, as the primary benchmark, captures the scaling-dependent performance
differences reported in Sections~\ref{sec:results_size} and~\ref{sec:results_data}:
it distinguishes configurations within a model family and across data fractions in
ways that the distributional measures do not resolve.
For instance, MUNIT achieves the single lowest per-configuration FID of any family
(medium/small: 163.7), while its best CPA MAE ($0.040~{\pm}~0.030$) remains
substantially above the best CycleGAN configuration ($0.008~{\pm}~0.007$),
underscoring that FID and CPA MAE each capture a distinct aspect of model behaviour.
