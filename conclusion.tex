\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Obtaining a Sirius Red stain for hepatic fibrosis assessment requires a separate tissue
section, consuming limited biopsy material and introducing spatial misalignment between
staining layers.
Virtual staining via unsupervised image-to-image translation offers a practical
alternative, yet no systematic benchmark had compared competing architectures for
H\&E~$\to$~SR translation or characterised how model capacity and training data volume
jointly affect translation quality in this setting.

We introduced an open whole-slide image dataset of paired H\&E and Sirius Red sections
from a mouse bile-duct ligation model of cholestatic liver disease~\cite{ghallab2025asbt},
providing the first dedicated resource for this translation task.
Building on this dataset, we conducted a full-factorial scaling study across six
unsupervised I2I architectures (CycleGAN, UNIT, MUNIT, DCLGAN, UVCGAN, and
CycleDiffusion), three generator capacities, and three training data fractions, yielding
54 independently evaluated configurations.
We evaluated all configurations with a combination of perceptual metrics (Patch-SSIM,
LPIPS, FID) and a task-based collagen proportionate area MAE, the latter exploiting the
paired tissue-block structure of the dataset to provide a biologically grounded
benchmark.
Finally, we trained deep ensembles of ten independently initialised models for the
best-performing configuration per family and constructed spatially resolved epistemic
uncertainty maps, constituting the first uncertainty analysis for unpaired virtual
histological staining.

The scaling study reveals that perceptual metrics saturate within a narrow band across
the majority of well-behaved configurations and correlate only moderately with CPA MAE
($\rho{=}0.55$--$0.64$), confirming that task-based evaluation is indispensable for
model selection in this domain.
CPA MAE spans over an order of magnitude across the 54 configurations ($0.008$--$0.171$),
with medium-capacity generators trained on the full dataset performing most reliably
across families.
MUNIT is the most stable architecture, maintaining a narrow CPA MAE range of
$0.040$--$0.059$ across all nine scaling conditions.
DCLGAN and UVCGAN exhibit catastrophic training failures at high generator capacity
under limited data, underscoring that over-parameterisation is as significant a
deployment risk as under-training.
CycleDiffusion produces competitive CPA MAE despite systematically lower Patch-SSIM
than GAN-based families, demonstrating that perceptual sharpness and biological
accuracy are not equivalent objectives.
