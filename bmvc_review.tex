\begin{abstract}
Virtual histological staining has advanced rapidly in recent years, yet
systematic benchmarks for unsupervised stain-to-stain translation remain scarce,
particularly for histochemical stains in liver tissue.
Sirius Red~(SR), one such stain used for collagen proportionate area~(CPA) measurement
in liver fibrosis staging, has received little attention as a virtual staining target.
No study has jointly characterised how model capacity and training data volume govern
Hematoxylin and Eosin~(H\&E) to SR translation quality, or quantified the predictive
uncertainty of such models, which is essential since visually plausible outputs in the
unpaired setting may not faithfully reproduce the underlying tissue structure.
We conduct a full-factorial scaling study across six unsupervised image-to-image~(I2I)
architectures (GAN-based and diffusion-based), three generator capacities, and three
training data fractions, and evaluate each configuration using perceptual metrics
and a task-specific CPA mean absolute error~(CPA~MAE).
Building on this, deep ensembles of $K{=}10$ independently seeded models are trained
for the best full-data configuration per family,
yielding spatially resolved uncertainty maps and the first systematic comparison of
epistemic uncertainty across unsupervised stain-to-stain architectures.
To support this benchmark, we release a paired H\&E and SR whole-slide image dataset
from a mouse bile-duct ligation experiment, the first open resource for unsupervised
H\&E~$\to$~SR translation in mouse liver tissue.
Our study demonstrates that perceptual metrics correlate only moderately with CPA~MAE
(Spearman $r_s = 0.55$--$0.64$), and that CPA~MAE itself ranges from $0.008$ to
$0.171$, a 21-fold span confirming that perceptual metrics alone cannot guide model
selection.
Ensemble variance separates families on a second axis beyond CPA~MAE.
DCLGAN combines low task error with the narrowest inter-seed spread (IQR $0.043$) and
the most informative variance signal.
CycleDiffusion records the lowest mean variance ($3.16$) but is uncorrelated with
cycle-reconstruction error.
\end{abstract}
