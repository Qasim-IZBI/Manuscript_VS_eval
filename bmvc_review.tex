\begin{abstract}
Comprehensive histopathological analysis requires multiple specialised stains, yet
each additional stain consumes a separate tissue section from a limited biopsy,
extends laboratory turnaround time, and introduces spatial misalignment between
staining layers.
Virtual staining, which predicts a target stain directly from a digitised H\&E slide,
eliminates this requirement and enables retrospective analysis from existing archives.
In liver pathology, Sirius Red~(SR) is the histochemical gold standard for collagen
proportionate area~(CPA) measurement and fibrosis staging, but no systematic benchmark
has compared unsupervised image-to-image~(I2I) architectures for H\&E~$\to$~SR
translation, characterised how model capacity and training data volume jointly govern
staining quality, or quantified the predictive uncertainty of such models.
We release an open whole-slide image dataset of paired H\&E and Sirius Red sections
from a mouse bile-duct ligation model~\cite{ghallab2025asbt} and conduct a
full-factorial scaling study across six architectures (CycleGAN, UNIT, MUNIT, DCLGAN,
UVCGAN, CycleDiffusion), three generator capacities, and three training data fractions,
yielding 54 configurations.
All configurations are evaluated with perceptual metrics alongside a task-based CPA
mean absolute error that exploits the paired tissue-block structure of the dataset
to provide a biologically grounded benchmark.
We additionally train deep ensembles for the best configuration per model family and
derive spatially resolved epistemic uncertainty maps, the first such analysis for
unpaired virtual histological staining.
Perceptual metrics saturate across well-behaved configurations and correlate only
moderately with CPA MAE (Spearman $\rho{=}0.55$--$0.64$), confirming that task-based
evaluation is indispensable for model selection in this domain.
CPA MAE spans over an order of magnitude ($0.008$--$0.171$) across the 54
configurations; MUNIT is the most stable architecture across all scaling conditions,
while DCLGAN and UVCGAN exhibit catastrophic training failures at high capacity
with limited data.
